Chapter 10

The AI PC Trap

Okay. Rant time, and then we move on.

In 2025–2026 every laptop company slapped "AI" on the box. AI PC. NPU TOPS. Powered by AI. You could not find a laptop that wasn't AI. Even the toaster they were giving away in the conference goodie bag was AI-enabled, as if I need my toast to be aware of what it is.

Here's the thing. The AI PC you bought at Best Buy has 135–230 GB/s of memory bandwidth. That's fine for a small model. That's fine for dictating notes. It's fine for the built-in assistant. But if you're running a 9B dense model playground, a code agent, long-context document analysis, or multi-agent workflows, you are out of your weight class. Physics does not negotiate.

The "AI PC" era is a branding era. It is not a performance tier.

Don't get me wrong — those boxes are good at what they're good at, which is low-power inference on small busy-work models. If you want a thing that quietly summarizes your email, sure, Lunar Lake, knock yourself out. But if you're deciding between that and "a real GPU," and this book is in front of you, then you already know which answer you want. Go buy VRAM and bandwidth, not marketing copy.

The corollary: NPU TOPS numbers are a distraction. They're a raw compute stat for a very specific kind of workload, and they don't tell you anything about memory bandwidth or about what models actually fit or about how any of it will feel in your actual use. Stop reading them. Print this chapter and tape it over the sticker.
