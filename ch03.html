Chapter 3

The Elephant in the Room (The KV Cache)

Alright. This is the chapter you skip at your own peril, because the KV cache is the #1 reason people buy the wrong hardware.

The KV cache is the model's working memory during generation. Every token you've fed it, every token it's generated — the model stores attention state (the K and V matrices) for those positions so it doesn't have to recompute the whole conversation on every new token.

The formula, so you can see why it explodes:

KV cache size ≈ tokens × layers × kv_heads × head_dim × precision × 2

That × 2 at the end is for keys and values. Yep.

Rule of thumb for an old-school Llama-style 7B MHA model in FP16: about 0.5 MiB of KV cache per token. That sounds fine until you do the math:

  • 4K token conversation → ~2 GiB of KV cache.
  • 32K token conversation → ~16 GiB. Just for the cache. Not weights. Cache.
  • 128K context on a beefier modern model → you need a planning meeting.

This is the whole secret: the model that "fits" on your card at empty prompt will OOM (out of memory) on the third long conversation you throw at it, because the KV cache grew quadratically in your mind's eye but linearly in reality, and reality always wins.

Bars = cache size from 1K to your selected context, 2K steps. Formula: 2 × layers × KV-heads × head-dim × bytes-per-value. 7B-class: 32 layers / 32 heads (8 GQA, 1 MQA) × 128. 13B-class: 40/40 (8/1) × 128. 70B-class: 80/64 (8/1) × 128.

Good news: modern models make this manageable:

  • GQA/MQA cut KV heads down from "tons" to "a couple." Big savings.
  • FP8 / INT8 KV cache is a real, production-usable feature in runtimes like vLLM in 2026. Cuts cache in half. Slight quality dip. Worth it.
  • Sub-8-bit KV cache (KIVI, KVQuant, the 2-bit / 4-bit research stuff) — works, but works the way surgery works. You need calibration, custom kernels, careful algorithms. It's NOT a checkbox in LM Studio. Do not flip it on for coding, tool use, JSON, or long-context retrieval and then get surprised when your answers stop making sense. Benchmark hard or don't touch it.

Also — important distinction that trips people up:

KV cache compression ≠ speculative decoding.

DFlash, DDTree (DTree), EAGLE, MTP — those attack latency by drafting tokens and verifying them. They make the model faster. They do not make the KV cache smaller. Your memory bill is the same. Your speed bill goes down. Different problems, different tools. People mash them together in comments constantly and it's maddening.

Now, keep that 0.5 MiB/token number in the back of your mind. Because in the next chapter, we're going to look at why when you spend those tokens changes everything about how fast the box feels.
