Chapter 5

VRAM Math — The Only Formula You'll Ever Need

Okay. Deep breath. This is the chapter you read out loud to yourself and then never have to think again.

People love to ask "can my GPU run this model?" like it's a mystery. It is not.

VRAM (GB) ≈ Parameters (billions) × (bits per weight ÷ 8)

That's it. That's the whole secret. Let me show you the numbers you need memorized:

  • FP16 / BF16 → 16 bits → ~2 GB per 1B parameters
  • FP8 / INT8 → 8 bits → ~1 GB per 1B params
  • 4-bit quants → ~4 bits → ~0.5 GB per 1B params

That's your literacy test. If you remember nothing else from this book, remember those three lines. Everything else is a variation on the theme.

GGUF variants sit in between, because the universe loves in-between things:

Q6_K~0.82 GB
Q5_K~0.69 GB

Ultra-aggressive quants go lower, at a cost — Q2 starts making your math and your JSON and your tool calls do the stanky leg. We'll talk about when that's an acceptable trade in Chapter 17.

Quick examples, because everyone loves examples:

  • A 7B model → 14 GB @ FP16, 7 GB @ FP8, ~3.5–4 GB @ 4-bit
  • A 13B model → 26 GB @ FP16, 13 GB @ FP8, ~6–7 GB @ 4-bit
  • A 70B model → 140 GB @ FP16, 70 GB @ FP8, ~35–40 GB @ 4-bit
  • A 405B model → 810 GB @ FP16, 405 GB @ FP8, ~200+ GB @ 4-bit (and you just understood why cloud exists)

Now the part that makes this actually useful: translating model sizes into GPUs people actually own. This is the table that ends arguments.

That 24 GB row is why the RTX 3090 is still the local-AI gold standard in 2026. It's ancient, it's loud, it draws 350 watts, and at 4-bit it runs the whole 35–40B class. That's where the value lives. The 5090 (32 GB) is its heir apparent. You don't need a data center to start. You need a 3090 and an attitude.

Wait, there's a tax though. Because "fits on paper" ≠ "fits in practice." Next chapter.
