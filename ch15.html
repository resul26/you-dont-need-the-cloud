Chapter 15

"It Runs" vs. "It Serves"

This is the chapter where we stop congratulating ourselves.

It runs means I got it to load, I got an answer, I screenshotted it. All good things. None of them are systems.

It serves means it handles the tenth concurrent user on a Tuesday. It means structured outputs parse reliably. It means KV cache doesn't thrash at sustained load. It means failures are graceful, observed, and recoverable. It means the p99 latency is close enough to the p50 that the demo doesn't embarrass you.

The distance between the two is:

  • Continuous batching and good scheduling. A request queue, not a pile.
  • Prefix caching, so your 4,000-token system prompt doesn't get recomputed every call.
  • Paged attention, so KV cache is fragmented sensibly and utilization is high.
  • Short-circuit failure paths — timeout policy, max tokens policy, concurrency limits, backpressure.
  • Observability — metrics, logs, traces. You cannot fix what you cannot see.
  • Realistic load testing before you call it done. Test on the model + hardware + concurrency you'll actually serve.

This is why the chapter 13 distinctions matter. It's also why "it fits" is different from "it serves." The box that fits 70B in 48 GB might not serve 70B well under concurrency. The one that fits 27B comfortably probably will.
