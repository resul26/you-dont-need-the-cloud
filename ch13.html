Chapter 13

Prefill, Decode, and Why Your Demo Lied to You

Back to this one more time, slowly, because if you get this wrong you'll benchmark wrong, tune wrong, and blame wrong.

Prefill processes your prompt. Builds the initial KV cache. Compute-bound. If your prompt is 20,000 tokens long, you pay 20,000 tokens of forward passes before a single answer token appears. It's why the experience of "paste a giant document and wait 25 seconds before anything happens" happens.

Decode generates the answer, one token at a time. Bandwidth-bound. Each token re-reads the whole model's weights plus the growing KV cache. Sequential. This is the "stream of words" feeling.

So what do you optimize?

  • Long prompt, short answer → prefill dominates → attention kernels, chunked prefill, fast prompt ingestion are the fix.
  • Short prompt, long answer → decode dominates → bandwidth, quantization, exact memory residency are the fix.
  • Lots of users → scheduler quality, continuous batching, prefix caching, pagination are the fix.
  • Long context → KV cache handling is the fix.
  • Agentic workloads (lots of small calls, many turns) → latency per loop dominates → speculative decoding, prefix caching, tight runtimes, KV reuse.

Your "I can demo it" moment is prefill + one decode. Your "I am a system" moment is sustained decode + concurrent prefill + growing KV + scheduler fairness under load. Benchmark accordingly.
