Chapter 21

Privacy Is Not Free

Headline: local improves privacy. Local does not automatically give you security.

Things that still threaten you in the local setting:

  • Malicious model files. Stick to safetensors, GGUF from reputable sources. Skip sketchy .bin files. Don't enable trust_remote_code casually.
  • Pickle-based weight loading. Avoid when possible.
  • Prompt injection through RAG. Retrieved documents can carry adversarial instructions.
  • Tool-call abuse. Schema-validate everything.
  • Secret leaks through logs, console output, or export paths.
  • Desktop app telemetry. Review it. Many apps phone home.
  • Browser extensions around your local tools.
  • Hallucinations in high-stakes settings. Local doesn't make results true.
  • License violations. Open-weight ≠ do whatever you want.
  • Data contamination during fine-tuning.

A workable baseline:

  • Load carefully. Prefer safetensors / GGUF from reputable sources. Skip sketchy files. No casual trust_remote_code.
  • Run with boundaries. Unprivileged user. Containers for agents. No network access when offline privacy matters.
  • Protect secrets. Keep credentials out of prompts and out of vector stores. Validate tool calls before execution.
  • Version what matters. Model, prompt, adapter, runtime, quantization. Log enough for debugging — not so much that the logs become a leak.

Local AI security is mostly boring operational discipline.

Boring is good. Boring is what saves you when someone hands you a "totally safe, bro, I compiled it myself" checkpoint.
