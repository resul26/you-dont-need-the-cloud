Chapter 20

Agents with Guardrails

A local chatbot that hallucinates is annoying. A local agent that hallucinates has access to your filesystem, your browser, your shell, your calendar, your email, your database — and once it's wired into the internet, it can leak secrets and wreck your week. You need real guardrails, not vibes.

Ahmad's four layers:

  • Scope the agent tightly. Give it the minimum directories, APIs, network access, and credentials it actually needs.
  • Constrain execution. Sandboxes, containers, least-privilege users, confirmations on destructive actions, schema-validated tool arguments.
  • Treat inputs as hostile. Documents, web pages, tickets, emails — any of it can contain prompt injection. Assume malicious instructions live in retrieved content.
  • Keep an audit trail. Log tool calls, model versions, prompts, and approvals. Don't dump secrets into logs.

Structured outputs (JSON schemas, constrained decoding, function signatures) help — but they are not a security boundary. They make output easier to validate. They don't prove the model understood the request, chose the safe action, or avoided injected instructions. For serious tool use, put policy checks outside the model.

The local advantage is real: you control the loop. But local isn't magic. Local means you own the security now too.
