Chapter 9

The 2026 Hardware Zoo (Real Boxes, Real Numbers)

Alright. Let me read you the menu as of mid-2026. Numbers come straight from Ahmad's articles — I checked them, they're solid. Memorize clusters, not individual numbers; Jensen moves fast but physics doesn't.

THE BANDWIDTH TIERS (a.k.a. the only chart that matters)

  • 1.8 TB/s class — RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell (96 GB), RTX 5090 (32 GB). This is the peak of what a normal person can plausibly buy.
  • 800 GB/s class — Mac Studio M3 Ultra (819 GB/s, up to 512 GB unified memory).
  • 450–650 GB/s class — MacBook Pro M5 Max (460–614), Mac Studio M4 Max (546), AMD Radeon PRO R9700 (640), Tenstorrent Blackhole p150 (512). Real workstation tier.
  • 250–300 GB/s class — DGX Spark (128 GB unified, 273 GB/s), Mac mini M4 Pro (273 GB/s), AMD Ryzen AI Max / Strix Halo (256 GB/s). This is "unified memory starts being interesting."
  • Below 150 GB/s class — MacBook Air M5 (153), Snapdragon X2 Elite (up to 228), Snapdragon X Elite (135), Intel Lunar Lake (136). Thin-and-light AI PC territory.

<threshold rules to remember>

The 2026 bandwidth ladder — filter it, then memorize the tiers

Below ~150 GB/s is thin-and-light. ~250–300 is where unified memory gets interesting. 450–650 is a serious workstation. 800+ is expensive, powerful, and fun. Capacity is not on this chart — that's the other axis. Capacity vs. bandwidth scatter lives in Chapter 8.

  • Below ~150 GB/s → thin-and-light. Fine for small models, assistants, edge. Not for serious work.
  • ~250–300 GB/s → unified memory starts being genuinely useful. Strix Halo, DGX Spark, M4 Pro mini.
  • ~450–650 GB/s → serious workstation. You can do real agentic work, meaningful local coding assistants, decent concurrency.
  • 800+ GB/s → expensive, powerful, and fun.

DISCRETE GPU REALITY

Still the decode kings when the model fits:

  • RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell — 96 GB @ 1792 GB/s. The dream.
  • RTX 5090 — 32 GB @ 1792 GB/s. The realistic dream.
  • RTX 4090 — 24 GB @ 1008 GB/s. Still excellent.
  • AMD Radeon PRO W7900 — 48 GB @ 864 GB/s. The "I want some non-NVIDIA options" answer.
  • AMD RX 7900 XTX — 24 GB @ 960 GB/s. Good bang-for-buck.
  • AMD Radeon AI PRO R9700 — 32 GB @ 640 GB/s. Good capacity, moderate speed.
  • Intel Arc Pro — rising. Fine to watch, but not default.

APPLE REALITY

The pitch: "not the fastest, but usable, and one box with a lot of memory."

  • Mac Studio M3 Ultra — 819 GB/s, up to 512 GB unified. The one-box dream for big models.
  • MacBook Pro M5 Max — up to 614 GB/s. The portable high end.
  • Mac mini M4 Pro — 273 GB/s. Small but real.

When Apple wins: you want quiet, one box, no drivers, no dual-GPU drama, and "stupid amounts of memory" matters more than raw tokens/sec. When Apple loses: you want maximum tokens/sec per dollar, or you want to run multi-agent workloads at scale. The MLX ecosystem is good, but its server software says, in writing, that it is not recommended for production. That's not a slur. That's their lawyers talking.

DGX SPARK

128 GB unified memory, 273 GB/s, full NVIDIA software stack. Bandwidth is unimpressive. The coherent memory plus the real NVIDIA stack is the point. NVFP4 support is there but immature. It's a developer appliance, not a race car. If that sounds like your situation, it probably is.

STRIX HALO / RYZEN AI MAX

256-bit LPDDR5X, up to 128 GB memory, ~256 GB/s bandwidth, exposes ~96 GB as GPU memory. The first genuinely legitimate x86 unified-memory play. Also why the little Framework Desktop boxes are interesting.

TENSTORRENT

Wormhole n300 at 24 GB / 576 GB/s. Blackhole p150 at 32 GB / 512 GB/s + 800G interconnect. Fully open-source stack. Ahmad says he's watching it and so am I. Open stacks are good for the world and Tenstorrent keeps showing up.

MULTI-GPU?

More GPUs ≠ linear scaling. Once you go past one card, you are now buying interconnect (PCIe vs NVLink vs RDMA), topology, sync overhead, and software maturity. Also, do not use llama.cpp or Ollama on multi-GPU setups — use vLLM or ExLlamaV3+. That's a direct quote and every official doc backs it.
