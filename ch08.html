Chapter 8

Capacity vs. Bandwidth — Two Different Animals

Okay, now we get into why hardware arguments on the internet are eternally wrong. It's because two things are being conflated.

CAPACITY decides what fits.

BANDWIDTH decides how fast it feels.

Local AI hardware = capacity × bandwidth × software stack

Think of it like this. Capacity is how big your garage is. Bandwidth is how wide the garage door is. You can have a monstrous garage and a door the size of a mailbox slot. You'll fit plenty of cars in there. You will never get them out. Or you can have a tiny motorcycle garage with a full-width industrial door. Whatever fits, fits instantly.

Fig 2 — Fit vs Speed: every box in the book, plotted
Hover a point for details. Upper-left = fast but cramped (discrete GPUs). Lower-right = roomy but chill (unified memory). The trade is the whole story of local hardware. Data: chapters 8–9 tables.

Here's the punchline, in big friendly letters:

  • A 32 GB RTX 5090 and a 96 GB RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell have the same bandwidth (~1792 GB/s). They live in totally different worlds once models get big, but a 7B model will run at roughly the same speed on both.
  • A Mac Studio M3 Ultra has 819 GB/s and can hold up to 512 GB of unified memory. A 180B-class dense model at 4-bit quant fits there. It doesn't fit on the 96 GB RTX PRO. But the RTX PRO will decode any model that does fit roughly twice as fast.
  • A DGX Spark has 273 GB/s and 128 GB of unified memory. It's not a speed demon. It IS a full NVIDIA stack appliance with coherent CPU/GPU memory, which makes it a genuinely useful developer box.

What does this mean in practice? Two different questions are being asked, and people keep answering the wrong one:

  • "Can I fit this model?" → That's a capacity question. Look at VRAM (or unified memory).
  • "Will it feel fast?" → That's a bandwidth question. Look at GB/s. For decode-heavy workloads, this is the number that matters.

Fit ≠ speed. "It runs" is a demo; "it serves" is a system design. The sooner you split these words, the fewer Reddit arguments you'll lose.
